02 / THE PERSON

Thinking about technology
through law and society.

I studied Law and Sociology at Koç University. Law taught me to examine the questions technology raises through rights and responsibilities; sociology taught me to look at what changes in people’s lives, institutions and relations of power. From my second year at university, my assistantship work explored how AI and emerging technologies affect law and society. These questions have held my interest for about ten years.

After qualifying as a lawyer, I began working with technology companies on technology policy, government affairs and corporate relations. Across more than seven years of professional experience, I have advised over 25 companies. Today, as an IAPP AIGP-certified AI governance professional, I draw on that experience in research, advisory work and teaching.

I run corporate work through STRATRI, the consultancy I founded. At TechLetter, I research technology developments, compare sources and share my own assessments. Trying a new tool and discussing what it could change in our lives still excite me.

Who is Nesibe Kırış Can?

I studied Law and Sociology at Koç University and am an IAPP AIGP-certified AI governance professional, with over seven years of experience in technology policy and advisory work. I deliver consulting and tailored training through STRATRI, the consultancy I founded, and publish independent analysis at TechLetter.

How do law and sociology inform your work?

Law brings questions of authority, responsibility and rights. Sociology brings questions about effects on people and institutions. When I examine an AI use case, I look at both the rules and the consequences in practice.

What is AI governance?

The structures and processes that establish who is responsible for decisions when AI is developed, bought and used, how risks are assessed and how use is monitored. Human oversight, data boundaries, controls and records are part of that work.

Are AI ethics, governance and legal compliance the same?

Ethics examines what is fair and acceptable. Compliance concerns applicable legal obligations. Governance brings these questions into roles, decisions and everyday controls. They inform each other, but their scope differs.

Where does AI governance start?

Start by understanding which tools are used, by whom and for what purpose. Then examine impacts, data, accountable people and existing controls. The scope should reflect the organisation’s uses and risks.

What do you publish at TechLetter?

Source-based analysis on AI governance, technology policy and social impacts, alongside reports and reference guides. TechLetter has 10.2k+ subscribers across 99 countries.
